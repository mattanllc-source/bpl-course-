## 1. Module purpose

C1 teaches you to decide, and document, whether a loan is business or consumer purpose, and which federal and state laws still reach it. Every later module rests on that decision, so C1 comes second in the core path, right after orientation.

## 2. Learning objectives

By the end of C1, you will be able to:

## 4. Depth targets: what to add, deepen, correct and cut

The current lesson is 1,588 counted words and measures 1.0 credit. An upper-undergraduate banking-law or real estate finance course would cover the items below.

### 4.1 Add

1. **The statutory layer above Reg Z.** TILA's own exemption (15 U.S.C. 1603(1); glossary item 6 cites it, checked 18 Nov 2026), the SAFE Act definition (12 U.S.C. 5102(9)) and the CFPA definitions (12 U.S.C. 5481(4)-(5)). Explain why federal originator licensing and CFPB UDAAP authority generally stop at consumer credit, while state law and the FTC Act may not.
2. **Comment 3(a)-6, business credit later rewritten.** This is the rule for Maple Run's month-14 DSCR refinance and for any cash-out. The lesson omits it.
3. **Comments 3(a)-9 and 3(a)-10, organizational credit and trusts.** Teach why some trusts are treated as natural persons, and why CORE-BOR-01 and CORE-KYC-08 bar trusts as borrowers.
4. **The Fair Housing Act boundary (needs analysis K6, §4 item 5).** 42 U.S.C. 3605(b)(1)(B) reaches RTL, DSCR and multifamily loans. Add disparate impact: *Inclusive Communities* (2015), HUD's 24 CFR 100.500 three-step burden shift, and HUD's January 2026 proposal to remove it. Teach that disparate-impact liability rests on the Act itself as the Supreme Court read it, so removing the HUD rule would not remove it [VERIFY: *Texas Dept. of Housing and Community Affairs v. Inclusive Communities Project*, No. 13-1371, 25 Jun 2015 (disparate-impact claims cognizable under the Fair Housing Act); Cornell LII; checked 22 Nov 2026]. How courts would apply a burden-shifting test without 24 CFR 100.500 stays open; do not predict it.
5. **The guarantor-as-applicant split.** Reg B treats guarantors as applicants only for 1002.7(d). *Hawkins* (2016) left the circuit split open: the Eighth Circuit below and the Sixth Circuit in *RL BB Acquisition v. Bridgemill Commons* (2014). Course researcher must open both circuit opinions before the lesson states their holdings. Justia blocked the Eighth Circuit PDF (HTTP 403) on 22 Nov 2026. **C1 owns this topic** (coverage review, 22 Nov 2026). It also takes C6's *Loper Bright* question: with *Chevron* deference gone, how will courts read 12 CFR 1002.2(e)? [VERIFY: *Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo*, Nos. 22-451 and 22-1219, 28 Jun 2024; Cornell LII; checked 22 Nov 2026]. Teach it as open, not settled. C6 applies CORE-BOR-05 and points back here.
6. **HMDA coverage thresholds in numbers.** 25 closed-end loans in each of the two prior years (1003.2(g)). Add a worked coverage test.
7. **State law, three more states.** Teach SLT-NC-01, SLT-IN-01 and SLT-CA-01 beside Ohio. Indiana's broker definition turns on collateral, so the entity rule does not settle it. California needs a lender licence even for business loans.

### 4.2 Deepen

1. **T2 Reg Z test.** Add three what-ifs on Maple Run: a member stays 3 weeks (owner-occupied, CORE-BP-02 stop); a sister moves in (Ohio "immediate family", OAC 1301:8-7-02(G)); the month-14 refinance pays out cash (3(a)-6 and CORE-BP-04).
2. **T3 law map.** Expand from 8 rows to 12: add SAFE Act, CFPA and UDAAP, Fair Housing Act and flood (preview of C9; 42 U.S.C. 4012a(b), glossary item 63). Add a column for "what triggers it": purpose, borrower type, collateral or lender type.
3. **T4 red flags.** Add the evidence ladder: what proves occupancy (lease, utility accounts, homestead filings) versus what only supports it (the certificate). Work both D-CORE-24 clearance paths on one fictional file each.

### 4.3 Correct

1. **"It cannot be fixed after closing"** (T1) is stated as a legal conclusion with no source. Rewrite with "generally" and cite comment 3(a)-6 and 12 CFR 1026.3(a) (Course Bible 5.6).
2. **"Our entity-only rule keeps it outside TILA anyway"** (T2, flips). True for our entity borrowers. But comment 3(a)-10 treats some trusts as natural persons. Add that caveat so learners do not over-generalize.
3. **"Institution thresholds apply"** (T3, HMDA) is vague. State the 25-loan test and its source.
4. **Section 1071 and FCRA cites** are carried from the lesson. Re-open both on the build date.
5. **Objective verbs.** "Spot" and "Map" are not in the LD-01 verb list. "Explain" and "Map" sit at levels 2. The new set uses approved verbs, six of seven at Apply or above.
6. **Quiz items break QAS rules.** Set A item 6 and Set B items 2, 4 and 7 are yes/no. All items are open response with revealed answers. Rebuild as four-option multiple choice.
7. **Adverse-action wording** is right but merges two rules. Split the 30-day notice (1002.9(a)(1)) from the business-credit options in 1002.9(a)(3).

### 4.4 Cut or move

1. **Detailed BSA/AML duties** (SAR timing, program elements). Keep one row in the law map. C7 teaches them in full.
2. **FCRA notice detail.** Keep the row; C6 and C7 teach it.
