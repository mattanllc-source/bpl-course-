## 1. Module purpose

C10 builds the underwriter's skill of reading the property state's law before a term sheet: licensing, rate and fee limits, foreclosure path and what a guaranty can recover. It follows C1 (purpose test) and C9 (documents), and it feeds C13 (default and REO) and C14 (the state-law section of the credit memo, CORE-AUTH-02).

## 4. Depth targets (what must be added, deepened, corrected or cut)

### Add

1. **Federal overlays (new T6, about 1,200 words).** The lesson treats state law as the whole story. Teach SCRA (50 USC 3953) and PTFA, and apply each to the three running deals. Name the automatic stay as the most common reason a CRE foreclosure stops, in one paragraph. **C13 owns bankruptcy** (stay, SARE 90-day rule, 506, 548), coverage review, 22 Nov 2026.
2. **Federal usury preemption.** Add DIDMCA §501 (12 USC 1735f-7a) as the federal layer for first-lien residential loans, with the 1980-83 state opt-out window. The opt-out status of Ohio, NC and Indiana is a look-up item.
3. **Usury consequences.** Teach that a usurious loan can forfeit interest or worse, and why that makes CORE-PRC-03 a LEGAL rule with no exception (CORE-EXC-03).
4. **Mortgage theories and redemption (new T1, about 1,800 words).** Lien versus title theory and equitable versus statutory redemption. These explain why Ohio and Indiana run through courts and North Carolina reopens sales with upset bids.
5. **Penalty doctrine.** The lesson says "the common-law penalty rule governs" with no doctrine. Teach the liquidated-damages test (Restatement (Second) of Contracts §356) and why California voids default interest on the full balance.

### Deepen

1. **Guarantor collectibility (T7).** WE5 says Dana Whitlow's $5,200,000 net worth "alone covers $680,000". Net worth is not cash. Re-work against her $760,000 post-closing liquidity (F-CORE-24), contingent liabilities and exempt assets. Take the liquidity figure from C6, which owns collectibility; C10 adds only the state exemptions and limits.
2. **Choice of law.** Add why a note's governing-law clause does not move foreclosure or anti-deficiency rules away from the property state.
3. **Prepayment.** Add perfect tender in time as the traditional default, and show that North Carolina rejects it (G.S. 24-2.4; *Hatcher v. Rose*). Then cover whether a state limits a premium. C8 owns the three premium designs (step-down, yield maintenance, defeasance); C10 names them and tests enforceability only. Policy uses step-down only (RES-DSCR-11; MF-TRM-06).

### Correct

1. **California usury cap.** The hook, T2 what-if and SLT-CA-01/-02 state a flat 10% cap. For non-personal loans the cap is the higher of 10% or 5% plus the SF Fed advance rate [VERIFY: Cal. Const. art. XV §1(2), secondary; checked 22 Nov 2026]. The "$850.00 a year of excess interest" claim holds only if 5% plus that rate is under 10%. The owner must correct the State Law Table first; the lesson follows it.
2. **Unsourced generalizations.** T1 "Most state mortgage license laws cover consumer loans only" and T2 "Most states exempt business loans in one of three ways" have no source. Anchor T1 on 12 USC 5102(9). Limit T2 to "the four states in our table show three patterns".
3. **Practice item 3 default interest.** It runs default interest from the missed due date. F-CORE-31 warns that the note may start it from notice. Add the note-language check to the answer.
4. **Assessment format.** Both quiz sets are short-answer. The project rule is multiple choice only, and QAS bars true/false and yes/no on exams. Items such as Set A 10 ("can we sue... ?") are yes/no. Rebuild per section 6.
5. **Indiana broker row.** Keep the lesson's open item. The owner corrects SLT-IN-01; the lesson then drops the conflict paragraph.
6. **Title line.** The lesson file has no `# C10` heading. Add it at build.

### Cut or move

1. **T5 recap paragraph** ("In short, Ohio, Indiana and North Carolina allow...") repeats the T4 table. Cut it (about 80 words).
2. **California guarantor material** stays here; C9 cuts its copy and points to C10.
