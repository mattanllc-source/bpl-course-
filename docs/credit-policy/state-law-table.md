## How to read it

The two column groups are split into two tables because the doc cannot hold 20 columns. Each state still has one row in each table. Read SLT-XX-01 for Maple Run-type loans and SLT-XX-02 for Cedar Court and Birchwood-type loans.

Three patterns hold in all four states:

- **Licensing turns on consumer purpose.** Residential mortgage licence laws cover loans for personal, family or household use. Business-purpose loans fall outside, except in California, where the finance lender law covers commercial loans too.
- **Usury turns on borrower and size.** Entity borrowers, business loans and larger loans are exempt in Ohio and North Carolina. California exempts licensed lenders and broker-arranged real estate loans instead.
- **Owner-occupancy triggers most protections.** Prepayment, late-fee and deficiency limits mostly apply to owner-occupied homes. A loan that slips into consumer use can pick them up (C1, CORE-BP-02).

## What this means for the running deals

- **Maple Run, Ohio (SLT-OH-01).** A loan to an LLC for a business purpose is usury-exempt (ORC 1343.01(B)(6)). No RMLA licence, because the house is for rent or resale and no owner or family will live there. Judicial foreclosure means a long, court-paced exit.
- **Cedar Court, North Carolina (SLT-NC-02).** $5,140,000 commitment ($4,340,000 at closing) to an LLC: an exempt loan under G.S. 24-9, so no rate or fee limit. Power-of-sale foreclosure is fast, but upset bids can reopen the sale.
- **Birchwood, Indiana (SLT-IN-02).** Non-consumer loan: no rate cap found. Judicial foreclosure with a 3-month wait. A full guarantee is collectable because deficiencies are allowed.
- **California (SLT-CA-01, -02).** The only state here needing a lender licence for business loans. A 10% usury trap applies unless a licensed lender or DRE broker is involved.
