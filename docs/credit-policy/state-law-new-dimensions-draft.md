## Why three tables, one per dimension

The live table already splits its columns because the doc cannot hold 20 columns. Each new dimension needs two or three sub-answers. Examples: before sale and after sale; attach date and rank. So each dimension gets its own table, with one row per SLT-XX-01 and SLT-XX-02 row. The owner can paste each table as a new group under the existing two. Where the -02 row matches the -01 row, it says "As SLT-XX-01", as the live table does.

- **C9 (O3, title).** The title review must ask whether work started before recording. In Ohio, North Carolina, Indiana and California, liens can relate back to work or furnishing on site. This supports a "no visible work" check and the mechanics' lien coverage on the title commitment.
- **C12 (T3, draws and liens).** Each state adds a draw condition. Ohio: an NOC on record, and draws paid in the 1311.14(B) order. North Carolina: a lien agent named and checked before each draw. Indiana: waivers only against payment; Class 2 risk on 1-2 unit rehabs. California: preliminary notices tracked, and a 75% payment bond if work began first.

- **Policy gap (owner question, not a new rule).** The Model Credit Policy has no mechanics' lien recording rule (course-frame summary, gap 10). No limit is proposed here.

**Why it matters to the lender.** If its activity goes beyond the safe harbor, an unregistered lender can be barred from suing or foreclosing in that state's courts until it registers. Delay and penalties follow.

**Course decisions it changes.**

- **C10 (T2, licensing and foreign qualification).** All four states have a lending and enforcement safe harbor, except Ohio corporations (none found). Teach the safe harbor as separate from the license check. Teach the exits: an in-state office (North Carolina) and repeated in-state activity.
- **Founder track.** Entity form matters in Ohio. An LLC lender has a statutory safe harbor; a corporation has none found. Opening an in-state office ends the North Carolina safe harbor.
- **C13 (judicial foreclosure).** In Ohio and Indiana, foreclosure is a court action. Registration status is a pre-filing check if the lender's activity goes beyond the safe harbor.
- **CORE-BP-03 (owner question, no new rule proposed).** The rule checks licenses only. Whether to add a registration check is for the owner to decide.
- **C9, C12.** No change.
