## B. Course by course

Verdicts compare CFI's public syllabus with our blueprint depth, not with today's shorter lessons. "Deeper" means our blueprint teaches the topic to a higher Bloom level or with primary-source law. "Thinner" means CFI has lessons on a part we lack.

### B1. Real Estate Fundamentals (3 h, 2 CPE, Level 3, Kyle Peterdy)

Objectives (quoted): "Identify differences and similarities between commercial real estate loan types"; "Interpret key underwriting parameters for commercial real estate lending"; "Compare equity lending and cash flow lending"; "Calculate lending ratios and appropriate loan amounts"; "Explain the timeline and end-to-end process of a commercial real estate transaction".

Syllabus: 7 sections, 42 items. Property types (multifamily, retail, office, industrial, special use, bare land). Reasons to own (owner-occupied versus investment). Purchase and sale agreement. Loan types. Site visit. Owner-occupied and income-producing transaction analysis, with a normalized income statement and "equity and cash flow methods". Seven underwriting parameters (environmental, property type, tenant quality, lease maturity, building condition, location, cash flow). Process and timeline. Loan advance and security registration. 3 interactive exercises, a midway check-in, course files and completed files, a qualified assessment.

| CFI topic | BPL module | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Loan types and property classes | C0 O1-O2; family R1, M1, CM1 | Covered similar. Core names BPL products; the property-type tour sits in the family tracks |
| Owner-occupied CRE analysis | None. C1 only uses owner occupancy for the purpose test | Not covered |
| Purchase and sale agreement | C2 O5 (life cycle), C11 (closing) | Covered thinner. No lesson reads a PSA |
| Site visit | None | Not covered |
| Seven underwriting parameters | C4 (cash flow, lease maturity), C5 (value), C8 (sizing), C9 (environmental, condition) | Covered deeper |
| Equity versus cash-flow lending | C8 O1-O2 (leverage versus DSCR, binding test) | Covered deeper |
| Lending ratios, loan amount | C3, C8 | Covered deeper |
| Process, timeline, advance and registration | C2 O5, C11 O1-O6 | Covered deeper |

### B2. How to Read a Commercial Real Estate Appraisal (2 h 30 min, 1.5 CPE, Level 3)

Objectives (quoted): "Understand why appraisals are such an important part of the real estate due diligence process"; "Identify key sections of the report and where to find the most important information"; "Complete a real estate appraisal checklist"; "Analyze multiple valuation approaches"; "Understand how the appraiser uses comparable properties"; "Identify what makes a good comparable property".

Syllabus: 8 sections, 43 items. Appraisal as a tool; estimated market value; who should appraise; report format. Letter of transmittal; intended user and purpose; completion versus effective date; expiration; executive summary; terms of reference. Property description; land use, controls and zoning; sale and ownership history; highest and best use. Cost, income (direct cap and DCF) and direct comparison approaches; comparables; reconciliation and signed certificate. Assumptions and limiting conditions. A section-by-section debrief. 6 interactive exercises, a midway check-in, a checklist demonstration.

| CFI topic | BPL module | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Why appraisals matter; market value | C5 O1 | Covered deeper (regulatory definition, premises of value) |
| Report anatomy: transmittal, intended user, dates, expiration | C5 O1, O4 (dates, validity factors, 120-day ceiling) | Covered thinner on anatomy; deeper on validity |
| Zoning and land use controls | None found in any blueprint | Not covered |
| Highest and best use | None found | Not covered |
| Three approaches, comparables, reconciliation | C5 O2-O3 | Covered deeper (DCF with reversion, band of investment, adjustment grid) |

### B3. How to Read a Lease and Analyze a Rent Roll (2 h, 2 CPE, Level 3, Lisa Dorian)

Objectives (quoted): "Outline the contents of a typical lease"; "Analyze the key components of a lease"; "Explore different lease types"; "Identify red flags in leases"; "Read and analyze a rent roll".

Tenant quality; term and maturity profile; lease rate; escalations; benchmarking rents; tenant incentives; net effective rate (two examples); terms and conditions. An 8-part lease walkthrough. Estoppel certificates. A 10-part rent roll build with an analysis tab. Downloads: slides, rent roll, walkthrough files, sample estoppel.

### B5. Real Estate Financial Modeling (6 h 30 min, 3 CPE, Level 5, Tim Vipond)

Objectives (quoted): "Outline the development timeline and corresponding financing tools"; "Outline the hallmarks of good Excel models"; "Explain the importance of clearly identifying model inputs and assumptions"; "Explain project finance funding tools"; "Identify the difference between project finance and corporate finance"; "Identify best practices of capital structure modeling in Excel".

Syllabus: 8 sections, about 66 items. Development timeline; project versus corporate finance; capital stack; modeling best practice. Cap rates and NOI with a sensitivity table and valuation matrix. Land loan (with a case-study assignment) and construction loan funding. A full development model: schedule, costs, absorption, closings, revenue, debt schedule, levered free cash flow and IRR. Joint ventures, disproportionate returns, a three-tier waterfall, IRR and NPV by investor. Output pages and sensitivity. Downloads: files, a completed cap-rate model, a completed full model.

## C. What CFI teaches that we do not

Ranked by importance for a US business-purpose real estate credit analyst. "Core" means C0-C14. "Family" means the planned R, M or CM tracks.

| Rank | Topic | Why it matters on a BPL desk | Belongs in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Construction and development process**: phases, key players, contract forms (stipulated sum, cost-plus, guaranteed maximum price), construction manager, construction types | Ground-up RTL and MF and COM bridge loans fund work in progress. Completion risk has the worst loss severity (needs analysis U6). CFI gives construction 24% of its program | Core: a real construction-risk chunk in C8 (today 10 minutes) and C12. | 14 | **Owner-occupied CRE and cash-flow (business) lending** | BPL lenders mostly lend to investors. Owner-user loans are a bank C&I product | Nowhere. Name it in C0 as out of scope |

| 16 | **Offer to lease** | A pre-lease document on new commercial leasing | Family: CM2 |
