## A. Who they are and how credible they are

**Who.** The NPLA calls itself "the national trade organization for nonbank real estate lenders." Its mission is "to protect and advance the interests of private lenders through legislative advocacy," plus education and collaboration. Members are lenders, capital providers, brokers and service firms. The office is in Marlboro, New Jersey.

**History and leadership.** The About page gives no founding date or leaders. A 2023 profile by its Executive Director gives the history. Leonard and Kathleen Rosen founded it in September 2019. In November 2022, Jonathan Hornik, Esq. and Private Lender Law acquired it. Private Lender Law is a practice group of the law firm LaRocca Hornik Rosen & Greenberg. Jonathan Hornik is Executive Director; Amy Kame is Managing Director. An advisory council of lenders exists. The 2023 list named Lima One, Roc Capital, RCN Capital, Sharestates and Northwind. Five committees exist: Best Practices, Ethics, Membership, Legislative and Technology. Member count is not published.

**Ownership matters.** A law firm group owns the association. The same group supplies its market data and the broker background check. That does not make the material wrong. It does mean the NPLA is not independent of the firms it describes.

**Credibility as a source.**

| Use | Fit | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Market practice (how private lenders actually underwrite) | Good | Written by a Best Practices Committee of working lenders. Practical and specific on valuation and budget review |
| Market data (rates, points, private-lender share) | Fair | Dated monthly. Partners named (SFR Analytics, Private Lender Law). Method, sample size and respondent counts are not published |
| Industry positions and advocacy | Good, as a stated position | It is the lenders' own voice. Wins are self-reported |
| Law or regulation | Poor | Not a primary source. One legal reference in its Code of Ethics names a statute that we could not find (see D5). Its glossary definition of a business purpose loan is narrower than Reg Z (see D1) |
| Neutral standard | Poor | Trade association. Its own standards say they are "not intended to direct the credit decisions of NPLA members" |

**How the course should cite them.** Use the source tag MARKET, as we do for lender guides. Name the document and its date, for example: "NPLA Best Practices Committee, *Underwriting Standards* (1-4 RTL), PDF dated 11 Sep 2025." Market figures carry "Market, as of <month year>" (quality bar 10.1). Never cite the NPLA for a legal rule. Pair every legal point with a statute or regulator and a `[VERIFY]` tag. Link to the PDFs; do not copy them. The glossary says "Distribution without permission is prohibited."

## C. Useful resources and how to use them

### C1. Underwriting Standards (item 1): the most useful resource

**What it says.** Four sections: collateral valuation, borrower evaluation, project review and pre-closing requirements. It is framed as best practice, not rules. "The guidelines below are also situational, as there are frequent tradeoffs between various credit factors."

Key passages:

- Valuation: "A compliant valuation does not automatically guarantee that the valuation is correct or accurate."
- "Comp fishing": "Determine if the selected comps may have been intentionally chosen to manufacture a desired value or ARV. Do this by identifying and analyzing which comps were omitted."
- Target ARV: "not every renovation = a complete remodel." Comps must not be "in superior condition to the proposed budget."
- Budget: "Is the budget sufficient to complete the project? If not, there are feasibility concerns. Is the budget TOO LARGE to complete the project? If so, there are fraud concerns." It also flags "front-loading" and line items that pay the borrower.
- Liquidity: funds in other LLCs "should not be included in a liquidity assessment unless legal documents can demonstrate" they are free to use.
- Permits: without them, "it is prudent to lend off the As-Is Market Value only."
- Insurance: deductible "generally no greater than 5% of the coverage"; insurer rated "AM Best A-VII or better or Demotech A or better" (the dash did not decode; read the PDF); policy term matches the loan "up to a year."

**Claims to check against a primary source.**

- "The credit score is used by 90% of the top U.S. lending institutions." No source is given. Do not repeat it.
- "Marital consent if lending to an individual in a joint property state." Check against Reg B's spousal-signature rule before teaching it. Our CORE-BOR-05 already cites 12 CFR 1002.7(d).
- Flood cover "where the structure is in a flood zone." Our CORE-INS-03 already handles this with a `[VERIFY]` tag.
- Loan documents "will vary by state." True in general. State specifics belong in C10 and the State Law Table, not here.

### C2. Code of Ethics (item 2)

**What it says.** Four duties: to the public, the borrower, the investor and the industry. Members "must review and accept the NPLA Code of Ethics upon being approved for membership and submit an annual statement attesting to its adherence." No enforcement process is described.

Key passages:

- Borrowers are told "the total cost and provisions of the loan," including rate, points, other fees and which fees are not refundable.
- "At no time will our members originate a loan based solely on the desire to see the recipient fail with the express intention of obtaining title to the property."
- "Fund draws for completed work in a timely fashion in accordance with loan document requirements."
- To whole-loan and fractional investors: disclose "known past borrower delinquencies," and conflicts. Named conflicts include lender ownership of appraisal or title firms, and "whether the loan is refinancing another delinquent loan originated or held by the lender."

### C3. Private Lending Glossary (item 3)

**What it says.** About 90 terms in seven groups: lender types, loan types, property types, servicing, loan documents, loan-tape data items and other terms. It aims at "uniformity in communication" across "legislators, the general public, and the lenders themselves."

Useful definitions for learners include: white label lender, table funder, correspondent, RTL (named as "a category of loans within the securitization market"), Dutch and non-Dutch interest, Day 1 loan amount, dry and wet funding, cross default, WAC and WAL. These suit the C0 market map and the planned capital-markets topic.

| Module | Quality-bar rows | How to use it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| C0 | 10.3 | Lender-type and funding-route vocabulary (table funder, correspondent, white label, aggregator) |

**Claims to check.** Do not teach three entries as written (D1, D3, D4).

**Worked check of the glossary's DSCR example (F-CORE-11).** The glossary says NOI of 100K and "total debt of 60K" gives "1.67%".

1. F-CORE-11: DSCR = NOI ÷ annual debt service.
2. Read "total debt" as annual debt service: $60,000.00.
3. DSCR = $100,000.00 ÷ $60,000.00 = 1.666666...
4. Displayed, half up: 1.67x.

So the right answer is 1.67x, not 1.67%. It is also a ratio to debt service, not to total debt. This makes a good "spot the error" multiple-choice item in C3 or C4.

### C4. Market reports (items 4 and 5)

**What they say.** Market, as of June 2026: RTL median rate 9.99%, DSCR median rate 6.88%; RTL median points 0.98, DSCR 1.00. 76% of RTL loans priced between 9% and 12%. Private lenders financed 16.4% of investor single-family purchases from 1 Jul 2025 to 30 Jun 2026. Market, as of December 2025 (Market Report page): bridge median rate 10.50% with 2.4 points; DSCR 7.00% with 1.7 points.

Trade press (National Mortgage Professional) reported the October 2026 report. It said 1.16% of outstanding private loans were in pre-foreclosure in October, against 1.10% in April. We did not open the NPLA's own October page. Confirm before use.

**Caveats.** Method, sample size and respondent counts are not published. Points medians differ between the December 2025 page (2.4) and June 2026 (0.98). That may be a real shift, or a change of data source. Treat each figure as one source, never the only one.

### C5. Broker Certification Course, Watchlist, member calls (items 8, 9, 11)

All gated. The course's six topic titles are public: "Private Lending Fundamentals; Best Practices in Private Lending; Preparing & Submitting Deals; Broker's Role & Agreements; Ethics, Fraud & Risk Prevention; Compliance & Background Check." No hours, exam, accreditation or price are stated. It is a broker credential, not a credit-analyst course. It does not compete with BPL Academy at university depth. It does use the word "Academy" (see D7).

## E. Recommendations, ranked

1. **Use the Underwriting Standards as a C5 and C8 learner reading.** Link to it, cite as MARKET with its date. Build one appraisal-review exercise on its valuation table and one budget-review exercise on its project review section. Cost: none.
2. **Fix how learners meet the business-purpose definition (D1).** Teach the Reg Z test first. Show the NPLA entry as an industry shorthand that is narrower than the rule. Cost: one C1 paragraph and one item.
3. **Use the NPLA monthly market posts as one dated source for RTL and DSCR pricing (quality bar 10.1 and 10.4).** Pair each with a second source. Record month and partner. Cost: none.
4. **Owner decision on insurance deductible and carrier rating (D8).** These are the clearest gaps the NPLA exposes. Decide whether to add a rule, with two sources.
5. **Use the Code of Ethics in C1 and C13.** Contrast "when suitable" with Reg B (D2). Use "loan to own" as an ethics grey case. Correct the advertising-law reference to the FTC Act (D5).
6. **Add the builder background report to the capstone ground-up file (D13).** It supports owner decision H2 without a new policy limit.
7. **Use the glossary errors as assessment material.** The DSCR example (D3) makes a strong "spot the error" item. Do not cite the glossary for any formula.
8. **Membership: not worth it now.** The Allied tier ($1,500, two years) gives the monthly data report and portal, but not the Watchlist or member calls. Associate is $6,000 a year; Corporate $10,000. The useful content for a course is already public: three PDFs and the monthly posts. Gated items (Watchlist, member directory) serve lenders, not course design. Revisit if the owner wants interviews with working lenders or the full data report as a cited source.
9. **Broker course: not worth buying for course design.** It is a broker credential with no stated hours, exam or accreditation. Reading its public topic list is enough. Check the "Academy" name overlap (D7) before launch.
10. **Do not cite the NPLA for law, licensing or state rules.** It publishes no state law summaries. C10 stays on statutes and the State Law Table.

## What to look up yourself

1. **The insurer rating string.** Open page 12 of the Underwriting Standards. Confirm whether it reads "A-VII" or "A VII".
2. **The NPLA's own October 2026 report.** Find it under Industry News. Confirm the 1.16% pre-foreclosure figure and its method.
3. **Market report method.** Ask the NPLA (info@nplaonline.com) for sample size, data source and respondent counts. Without them, keep a second source for every figure.
4. **Protected classes (D6).** Read 15 U.S.C. 1691(a) and 42 U.S.C. 3605. List which classes each covers.
5. **Rating scales (D4).** Read one agency's published rating definitions, for example Morningstar DBRS or KBRA. Note the lowest categories.
6. **The "Academy" name (D7).** Search the USPTO trademark database for "Private Lending Academy" and "BPL Academy".
7. **Reg B spousal signatures and marital consent (C1).** Re-read 12 CFR 1002.7(d). Check how it treats signatures needed under state law for secured credit.
