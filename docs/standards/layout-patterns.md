## A. Quality Matters Higher Education Rubric

**Edition.** The Seventh Edition is current. QM has released "Version 2.0 of the QM Higher Education Rubric, Seventh Edition". These updates "do not change the underlying Standards or how they are applied." No eighth edition is mentioned. (qualitymatters.org rubric page.)

**Structure.** "A set of eight General Standards and 44 Specific Review Standards." Each specific standard is worth 3, 2 or 1 points. The total is 101 points (sum of the public list below).

**Essential standards.** "Essential Standards (3-point Specific Review Standards) must be met during the review." There are 22 of them (counted from the public list). Certification also needs "an overall score of 85% of the points possible."

**Copyright note.** QM's public list says it "may not be copied or duplicated without written permission." So the standards below are short paraphrases, not the official wording. Read the exact text at the source PDF.

**Fit note.** The rubric targets instructor-led college courses. Some standards (instructor contact, learner introductions, institutional services) need adapting for a solo, self-study course. I flag these as "adapt".

## B. Patterns that recur across the best lesson pages

Each pattern lists where it was seen and how BPL Academy should apply it. "Seen" means on a fetched page in section F.

**P1. Learning outcomes open every lesson, as short imperatives.**
- Seen: OpenStax 8.3 and 8.5 open with "By the end of this section, you will be able to:" and four bullets, e.g. "Create an amortization schedule." CFI Commercial Mortgages has a "Learning Objectives" lesson second in the course.
- Apply (lesson page, module overview): Start each lesson with 3-4 one-line outcomes. Keep formal Bloom objectives with IDs in a collapsible block below. Meets QM 2.3.

**P2. A title block with one-line summary, byline, date and read time.**
- Seen: CFI DSCR: H1, a one-line subtitle, "Written by CFI Team", "Published January 29, 2020", "Read Time 7 minutes".
- Apply (lesson page, formula page): Under each title show a one-line summary, "about N min" and "Last reviewed <date>". Meets QM 4.4. Avoid CFI's stale-date problem: show a review date, not only a publish date.

**P3. A table of contents that mirrors the H2 headings, as anchors.**
- Seen: CFI DSCR lists 8 anchor links matching its sections in order.
- Apply (lesson page, formula page): Keep the "On this page" list. Cap it near 8 items (benchmark rec 6 already says so).

**P4. A boxed "Key Highlights" summary near the top.**
- Seen: CFI DSCR has a "Key Highlights" box with 4 bullets in its first section. The CFI cap rate page does not have one, so CFI is inconsistent.
- Apply (lesson page, formula page): Put a 3-4 bullet box after the definition. Use it consistently on every page. Pair it with a closing summary (P10).

**P5. Definition first, then formula, then "how to calculate".**
- Seen: CFI DSCR order: "What is...", "...Formula", "How to Calculate...". CFI cap rate: "What is...", "Cap Rate Formula", then "Practical Example". OpenStax 8.3 puts concept prose before the equation.
- Apply (formula page): Use the anatomy in section C.

**P6. Variables defined in a "Where:" list right under the formula.**
- Seen: CFI DSCR and cap rate both follow the formula with "Where:" and one bullet per variable. OpenStax 8.3 defines variables inline instead, with no separate key.
- Apply (formula page): Always use a "Where:" list with units (dollars, percent, days). The OpenStax inline style is harder to scan.

**P7. Formulas as text, not images.**
- Seen (negative): On both CFI pages the formula and worked-example tables did not appear in extracted text. That suggests images *(inferred)*. OpenStax shows formulas as text with equation numbers (8.31 to 8.36).
- Apply (formula page, lesson page): Render formulas as accessible text or MathML. Number them by formula ID, not page order. Meets QM 8.3.

**P8. Worked examples with full substitution, then a schedule table with totals.**
- Seen: OpenStax 8.3 shows a $32,000, 36-month, 6% loan. It substitutes r = 0.5%, n = 36 to get about $973.50. Table 8.4 has columns Month, Payment, Interest, Principal, Remaining Balance, and a Total row. A "(Rounding)" label appears in the totals.
- Also seen: OpenStax 8.3 text says "Dividing both sides by 32.781" while the equation shows 32.871. Even top sources ship arithmetic errors.
- Apply (lesson page, formula page): Keep our Formula > Inputs > Substitution > Result layout. Generate every number from `fixtures.json` so text and tables cannot disagree. Show a rounding-difference line in totals.

**P9. Truncated long tables with a continuation row.**
- Seen: OpenStax Table 8.4 shows rows 1-5, a "…continued…" row, then rows 33-36 and a Total row. Captions sit below tables.
- Apply (lesson page): Show the first and last rows of long schedules inline. Link the full schedule in the workbook.

**P10. A closing summary and an "Additional Resources" list of sibling topics.**
- Seen: CFI cap rate ends with "Cap Rate Summary" then "Additional Resources". CFI DSCR ends with "Additional Resources" listing six related links and "See all" by topic.
- Apply (lesson page, formula page): End with "Key takeaways", then "Related formulas" (LTV, LTC, DSCR, debt yield). Then the required self-check, "What to look up yourself" and "How to check it".

**P11. Interpretation and limits get their own section.**
- Seen: CFI cap rate "Why is the Capitalization Rate Important?" and the caution against using cap rate alone. CFI DSCR "Common Questions" with question headings ("Why use EBITDA?") and "Other Common DSC Adjustments".
- Apply (formula page): Add "How to read the result" and "When it misleads" sections. Use question headings. Tie any threshold to a Model Credit Policy rule ID, never an unsourced norm.

**P12. Calculator and spreadsheet steps as tables and exact syntax.**
- Seen: OpenStax 8.5 keystroke tables with columns "Step, Description, Enter, Display". Excel syntax inline, e.g. "=PV(rate,periods,payment,0,0)", then each argument explained. Spreadsheet screenshots are captioned and have alt text describing cells and formulas.
- Apply (formula page): Add an "In Excel" block with the exact formula and an argument table. Captioned workbook figures need alt text. (Matches benchmark rec 7.)

**P13. Module = sections of short items, with interleaved exercises.**
- Seen: CFI Commercial Mortgages: 9 sections, about 57 items. Most sections end with "Interactive Exercise 1" to "6". It has a "Mid-Way Check-In", a case-study section and a final "Qualified Assessment". Curriculum columns read "Lesson, Multimedia, Exams, Files".
- Apply (module overview, lesson page): End each lesson chunk with a short MC check. Add a mid-module check-in. Close each module with a running-deal case section before the quiz.

**P14. A "Download Course Files" item at the very start.**
- Seen: CFI Commercial Mortgages section 1 is "Introduction", "Learning Objectives", "Download Course Files".
- Apply (module overview): Put downloads (blank and completed workbook, PDF formula sheet) as the third item of every module. (Matches benchmark recs 3 and 17.)

**P15. Syllabus shows counts by item type and time per item.**
- Seen: Coursera Wharton finance: "Week 1: Time Value of Money", "Module 1", 3 hours. "What's included" tallies "4 videos, 6 readings, 2 assignments". Expanded: "4 videos•Total 41 minutes", items like "Compounding•7 minutes", "Practice Quiz #1•30 minutes", "Module 1 Quiz•30 minutes".
- Apply (home/catalog, module overview): Show per-module counts (lessons, worked examples, checks, quiz items) and per-item minutes. Label practice and graded quizzes differently. Meets QM 1.2 and 3.4.

**P16. Course facts panel: level, length, credits, field, prerequisites.**
- Seen: CFI Commercial Mortgages: "Level 4", "5h", "100% online and self-paced", "NASBA CPE Credits: 3", "Field of Study: Finance", prerequisites "Excel and critical thinking". FAQ states "at least 80% to pass".
- Apply (module overview, home/catalog): Show a facts panel on every module card. Add the sponsor line only once registered. Meets QM 1.7 and 3.2.

**P17. Frequent short activities, plus decision-from-the-seat cases.**
- Seen: HBS Online blog: learners are "presented with an activity every three to five minutes". Types include polls, rating and ranking, matching, and short reflections. Cases ask you to "assume the perspective of a business leader facing a real challenge," then show what the leader did.
- Apply (lesson page, quiz): Aim for a check every 5-10 minutes of reading *(our target, not observed)*. Use "You are the credit officer on Cedar Court. What do you do?" items, then reveal the policy answer. Keep them MC.

**P18. Prev/next links at the end of every section; a persistent contents control.**
- Seen: OpenStax places "Previous" and "Next" after the content. Its header has Contents, Highlights, Print, Search and text size.
- Apply (lesson page): Keep prev/next at the bottom. Add a Print control and keep Search in the header. Meets QM 8.1.

**P19. Phones are designed, not shrunk (M2c, 23 Nov 2026).**
- Seen: our own 390 px review. Top bar, notice strip and bottom bar took about 200 of 844 px; cards sat inside cards; the quiz opened on its rules.
- Apply (every page under 720 px): The top bar keeps Menu, the wordmark and Search; Audit mode and the theme sit in the course menu. The bar slides away while reading down and returns on scroll up, at the top and when it takes focus. The training label shows once, in the page flow at the foot of the content, not in a pinned strip. Desktop keeps the notice strip.
- Apply (under 600 px): Outer cards become edge-to-edge sections with 16 px gutters; inner tables and callouts keep their borders.
- Apply (lesson, quiz): One Next at a time. The bottom bar's Next names its page ("Next: Underwritten NOI") and the pager keeps Previous. While a quiz is taken, its Previous and Next own the bottom bar; the module bar returns on the review. The quiz opens on the question and its options; the rules fold into "How this quiz works". The course assigns the form (Form A first, the other form on each retake), so learners see no form tabs.
- Apply (interactive desk): Sliders get a 44 px thumb and labelled − and + buttons. On phones the working shows as stacked steps.

## C. Formula presentation standard

Based on CFI DSCR, CFI cap rate, and OpenStax 8.3 and 8.5. Investopedia could not be fetched. One page per formula ID, top to bottom:

1. **Title block.** Formula name, formula ID chip (`F-CORE-14`), one-line plain-English summary, "about N min", "Last reviewed <date>". (P2)
2. **Training label.** "Model policy for training - not for live lending decisions."
3. **What it is.** Two or three sentences. Say what question the formula answers for a credit officer. (P5)
4. **Key highlights box.** Three or four bullets. Include where it shows up in the deal flow. (P4)
5. **The formula.** One display line in accessible text, using words, not single letters. Example: `DSCR = Net operating income ÷ Annual debt service`. (P7)
6. **Where:** One bullet per variable, with its definition, unit and source document (rent roll, note, appraisal). (P6)
7. **Conventions.** Rounding (half up, display only), day count (Actual/360), and any policy rule that governs it, cited by rule ID.
8. **Worked example.** Use a running deal (RD-RES-01, RD-MF-01 or RD-COM-01). Show Inputs table > Substitution > Result, every number from fixtures. (P8)
9. **Schedule or reconciliation table,** if the formula produces one. Show the first and last rows, a Total row and a rounding line. Caption below. (P8, P9)
10. **In Excel.** Exact formula syntax and an argument table. Link the workbook cell. (P12)
11. **How to read the result.** Direction (higher or lower is safer) and the Model Credit Policy limit by rule ID. Never cite a market "norm" without a source. (P11)
12. **When it misleads / Common slips.** Question headings, e.g. "Why not use 365 days?" (P11)
13. **Quick check.** Two or three MC items with a rationale for each option.
14. **Key takeaways.** Three bullets. (P10)
15. **Related formulas.** Sibling links by family (P10). Then "What to look up yourself" and "How to check it".

## D. Reference shelf standard

What the fetched sources do:

- **Glossary.** OpenStax ships a per-chapter "Key Terms" page. It is alphabetical, one term per line with its definition. Chapter 8 has 14 terms, e.g. "annuity": "a stream of regular, periodic payments to be received or paid." It sits between "Summary" and the next chapter-end page.
- **Topic hubs.** CFI groups resource articles under topic breadcrumbs ("Home > Resources > Commercial Lending > Debt Service Coverage Ratio"). It ends each with "See all" links by topic (Accounting, Commercial Lending, Financial Modeling).
- **Templates.** CFI lists a "Debt Service Coverage Ratio Template" under Additional Resources. It is gated: "Create a Free Account to unlock this Template".
- **Course files.** CFI puts "Download Course Files" in each course's introduction section.
- **Formula sheets.** No fetched source showed a standalone formula sheet. This is a gap to fill, not a pattern to copy.

**Recommended BPL Academy shelf.** Four tabs, each searchable and printable:

1. **Glossary.** Alphabetical with a letter jump bar. Each entry: term, one-sentence definition, "Used in" module links, related terms. Also give a per-module "Key terms" list (OpenStax pattern).
2. **Formula sheet.** Grouped by family, then topic (interest, sizing, coverage, closing). Each row: formula ID, name, one-line formula, units, link to its formula page. Offer a PDF version.
3. **Policy rules.** Grouped by family and topic code, matching rule IDs (`CORE-AUTH-01`). Each row: ID, plain-English rule, limit, modules that teach it. Label every page as model policy.
4. **Downloads.** Per module: blank workbook, completed workbook, formula sheet PDF, print view. Never gated.

Shared rules: one canonical page per term, formula or rule; everything else links to it. Show "Last reviewed" on each shelf. Put the snapshot date from `docs/SNAPSHOT.md` on printed sheets.

## E. What award judges look for

**Brandon Hall Group, HCM Excellence Awards, Learning and Development (2026 form, "Best Competencies and Skill Development").** Judges score "on a scale of 1-6" on each criterion:

- [ ] **Fit to the Needs.** Aligned "with the business/organizational needs and situation."
- [ ] **Design of the Program.** Designed to close the stated skill gaps.
- [ ] **Delivery of the Program.** Delivered "effectively, and easy to access and apply."
- [ ] **Measurable Benefits.** The program "produced measurable benefits and outcomes."
- [ ] **Overall.** Shows "a positive impact for the learners and organization."
- [ ] Entry tips from the form: "Focus on specific details and measurable outcomes rather than promotional language." Give working course links valid for a year.

**Learning Technologies Awards 2026 (UK), "Excellence in the design of learning content".**

- [ ] "The design has been aligned to a clearly identified performance need."
- [ ] Media are "used in a way that encourages and reinforces learning."
- [ ] "Interactivity has been used in a way that encourages and reinforces learning."
- [ ] "The design represents the best possible solution given the constraints and issues encountered."
- [ ] "The reaction of learners and other key stakeholders to the design has been positive," backed by "appropriate quantitative and qualitative data."
- [ ] Shortlisted entries present to judges. It "may be strongly beneficial if the judging panel can see your submission in action."

**What this means for BPL Academy.** Both programs judge evidence, not looks. Start collecting now:

- [ ] A written performance need (what a new credit analyst gets wrong today).
- [ ] Pre- and post-test scores per module.
- [ ] Time-to-competence or error rates on the running deals.
- [ ] Learner feedback, even from pilot users.
- [ ] A design rationale explaining what was ruled out, and why.

- Open the CFI DSCR page and confirm the 8 table-of-contents items and the "Key Highlights" box.
- Open OpenStax 8.3 and confirm Table 8.4 columns and the 32.781 versus 32.871 mismatch.
