## 1. Hook (5 minutes)

Month 30 at Birchwood. The replacement tenant has walked away. NCF has fallen to $300,000, and the $24,200 monthly interest bill goes unpaid.

Dana calls to "talk about options". What you say on that call can waive our rights. What you choose next can cost or save hundreds of thousands of dollars. This module is about choosing well, in the right order.

## 2. Objectives

By the end of C13, you will be able to:

1. **Respond** to a default in the right order: notice, rating, pre-negotiation letter (CORE-DEF-01; CORE-SVC-03, -07; F-CORE-31).
2. **Compare** resolution options by net loss, including time (CORE-DEF-02; F-CORE-05, -23).
3. **Structure** a forbearance or modification (CORE-DEF-03; COM-BR-02; F-CORE-22).
4. **Choose** between a deed in lieu and foreclosure, and decide on a receiver (CORE-DEF-04, -05; SLT foreclosure column).
5. **Evaluate** what the guaranty adds after state deficiency rules (CORE-DOC-01, -02, -04, -05).
6. **Record** REO and plan its sale (CORE-DEF-06; F-CORE-42).

## 3. Teach

### T1. The first 30 days (Objective 1, 15 minutes)

A missed payment starts a sequence. Do it in this order.

1. **Send written notice** of default under the loan documents. Default interest runs as the note allows (CORE-SVC-03).
2. **Re-rate the loan** and put it on the watch list (CORE-SVC-07).
3. **Check the state row.** Default interest, foreclosure method and deficiency rules all come from the State Law Table (C10).
4. **Get a pre-negotiation letter signed** before any workout talk (CORE-DEF-01).

**Why the letter matters.** Fannie Mae tells its servicers not to have any workout discussion without a signed pre-negotiation letter. The reason: borrowers often claim that talks changed the loan, and courts may agree [VERIFY: Fannie Mae pre-negotiation form instructions; checked 20 Nov 2026]. The letter says talks change nothing until a written agreement is signed. Guarantors sign it too.

**New rule CORE-DEF-01.** Before any workout discussion, the borrower and every guarantor sign a pre-negotiation letter. It states that talks, partial payments and delays waive no rights, and that only a signed written agreement changes the loan.

**Worked example 1: Birchwood, 30 days in default (F-CORE-31).**

1. Formula: default interest = balance × (note rate + 5.00%) ÷ 360 × days.
2. Inputs: $3,520,000; 8.25% + 5.00% = 13.25%; 30 days.
3. Substitution: $3,520,000 × 13.25% ÷ 360 × 30.
4. Result: $38,866.67, which is $14,666.67 more than normal interest of $24,200.00.

Indiana check: no rate cap found for non-consumer loans (SLT-IN-02). Rating: 4 Substandard. The loan is in default and the exit no longer covers it (CORE-SVC-07).

### T2. Comparing the options (Objective 2, 25 minutes)

US bank regulators say prudent workouts are often in the interest of both lender and borrower. Their 2023 policy statement covers short-term accommodations as well as longer workouts [VERIFY: Interagency Policy Statement on Prudent CRE Loan Accommodations and Workouts, 29 Jun 2023; checked 20 Nov 2026]. We follow the same idea. A workout is not a favour to the borrower; it is chosen when it loses us less.

The options, from lightest to heaviest:

| Option | What it is | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Short-term forbearance | We hold off enforcing for a set time, on conditions | The problem is only delayed |
| Modification | Changed terms, usually with a paydown | Re-default later, after more lost time |
| Deed in lieu | The borrower deeds us the property | We take title subject to junior liens |
| Foreclosure | A court or trustee sells the property | Time and cost; the borrower may file bankruptcy |

**New rule CORE-DEF-02.** Every resolution memo compares the options by F-CORE-23 net loss, counting lost interest, legal, receiver and sale costs, and income collected. Guarantor recovery is shown separately. The option with the lowest expected loss is recommended, unless the memo explains why not.

**Worked example 2: Birchwood, foreclosure or deed in lieu.** C13 what-if facts: balance $3,520,000; note rate 8.25%; as-is value $3,400,000; sale costs 3%; NCF $25,000 a month collected during the hold.

| Line | Foreclosure (Indiana, judicial, with receiver) | Deed in lieu |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Time to title plus sale | 18 months (540 days) | 9 months (270 days) |
| Lost interest, $806.67 a day (F-CORE-05) | $435,600.00 | $217,800.00 |
| Legal, receiver, title and environmental | $90,000.00 | $40,000.00 |
| Sale costs, 3% of $3,400,000 | $102,000.00 | $102,000.00 |
| Total costs | $627,600.00 | $359,800.00 |
| Recovery: price + NCF collected | $3,400,000 + $450,000 = $3,850,000 | $3,400,000 + $225,000 = $3,625,000 |
| Loss: $3,520,000 + costs − recovery | $297,600.00 | $254,800.00 |
| Severity (F-CORE-23) | 8.5% | 7.2% |

The deed in lieu saves $42,800 on the property. But that is not the whole answer; see T5 for the guaranty.

### T3. Forbearance and modification (Objective 3, 15 minutes)

A forbearance buys time for a plan that can work. A modification changes the loan so the plan can work. Neither should be granted to a sponsor who cannot or will not support the asset.

**New rule CORE-DEF-03.** A forbearance or modification is written and time-limited. It needs:

- a signed pre-negotiation letter and guarantor reaffirmation;
- all net operating income paid to us during the period;
- current taxes and insurance; and
- a business plan with dated milestones.

Default interest may be waived only on full performance. Approval follows the authority matrix for the original loan amount.

**Worked example 3: Birchwood modification with a paydown.** Dana has $850,000 of liquid assets (deal file). She offers to pay down $300,000.

1. New balance: $3,520,000 − $300,000 = $3,220,000.
2. Interest a year: $3,220,000 × 8.25% = $265,650.
3. Coverage on in-place NCF: $300,000 ÷ $265,650 = 1.13x, above the 1.00x extension test (COM-BR-02).
4. Fee: $3,520,000 × 0.25% = $8,800.00 (F-CORE-22, on commitment).

Terms: 12-month extension; cash sweep stays on (COM-GEN-12); Dana reaffirms her guarantee; leasing milestones every quarter. If she meets them, we lose nothing. If not, we foreclose a year later with $300,000 less at risk.

### T4. Deed in lieu, foreclosure and receivers (Objective 4, 20 minutes)

**Deed in lieu.** It is fast and cheap, but it does not clear the title. Junior liens survive a deed in lieu, though a foreclosure would wipe them out [VERIFY: WealthManagement.com; Blank Rome; checked 20 Nov 2026]. So title diligence comes first.

**New rule CORE-DEF-04.** Accept a deed in lieu only after all of these:

- a title, UCC and judgment search showing no junior liens, or each one released;
- an estoppel affidavit from the borrower;
- anti-merger wording in the agreement and deed, with a non-merger title endorsement where available; and
- an environmental review.

Otherwise, foreclose.

**Receivers.** In a judicial foreclosure, the borrower keeps control and the rents until sale unless a receiver is appointed. A receiver collects rent, pays taxes and keeps the property running [VERIFY: National Law Review, Wisconsin receivers; checked 20 Nov 2026].

**New rule CORE-DEF-05.** In judicial-foreclosure states, ask for a receiver on multifamily and commercial loans when we file. The loan documents must allow it. In a power-of-sale state, ask for one if the sale will take longer than the property can safely run unmanaged.

**Bankruptcy.** A bankruptcy filing stops foreclosure at once (the automatic stay, 11 U.S.C. 362) [VERIFY]. Expect it, and get counsel involved early.

### T5. What the guaranty adds (Objective 5, 10 minutes)

The best property outcome is not always the best total outcome. Ask: after the sale, can we collect the shortfall from someone?

**Worked example 4: Birchwood, with Dana's guarantee.**

| Path | Property loss | Can we chase Dana? | Likely total loss |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Modification with paydown | $0 if the plan works | Guarantee reaffirmed | Lowest |
| Deed in lieu | $254,800.00 | Only if the agreement keeps the guarantee alive for the shortfall | $0 if agreed; $254,800.00 if released |
| Foreclosure | $297,600.00 | Yes: Indiana allows a deficiency (SLT-IN-02) | Near $0 if Dana pays; net worth $5,200,000 |

Two traps from C10. First, if we consent to waive Indiana's 3-month wait, no deficiency is allowed (IC 32-29-7-5). Second, in California the guarantee survives a trustee's sale only with Civ. Code 2856 waivers (CORE-DOC-05).

**Recommendation.** Offer the modification first (CORE-DEF-03). If Dana refuses, take a deed in lieu only with a written guarantee of the shortfall. Otherwise, foreclose with a receiver and pursue the deficiency.

### T6. REO (Objective 6, 10 minutes)

Property we take back is real estate owned (REO). Banks record it at fair value less cost to sell when they take title. Any excess of the loan over that amount is a loss [VERIFY: Federal Reserve OREO Q&A; FDIC FIL-62-2008; checked 20 Nov 2026]. We follow the same rule.

**F-CORE-42: REO carrying value and charge-off (SUGGESTED, new).**

1. Formula: carrying value = min(recorded loan, fair value − cost to sell); charge-off = max(0, recorded loan − (fair value − cost to sell)).
2. Inputs (Birchwood, foreclosure path): $3,520,000; $3,400,000; $102,000.
3. Substitution: $3,400,000 − $102,000 = $3,298,000; $3,520,000 − $3,298,000.
4. Result: carry at $3,298,000.00; charge off $222,000.00 at title.

**New rule CORE-DEF-06.** At title: a new appraisal; record the REO under F-CORE-42; secure, insure and manage the property. List it with a broker under a written sale plan. Re-test value each quarter and write it down if fair value less cost to sell falls. Every offer goes to the approver for the original loan.

## 5. Practice (40 minutes)

Use RD-MF-01 Cedar Court, North Carolina. C13 what-if facts: the borrower stops paying at month 30. Balance $5,140,000; note rate 9.00%; as-is value $5,000,000; sale costs 3%; NCF $35,000 a month. The loan is non-recourse with carve-outs (CORE-DOC-02). Foreclosure takes 9 months to title and sale (270 days); a deed in lieu takes 6 months (180 days). Legal costs: $60,000 foreclosure, $35,000 deed in lieu. Title search shows a $40,000 mechanic's lien.

1. List your first four steps, with rule IDs.
2. Compare foreclosure and deed in lieu by F-CORE-23.
3. Which do you choose, given the mechanic's lien?
4. Can you pursue the guarantors for the loss?
5. Record the REO with F-CORE-42.

**Answer key.**

1. Written notice and default interest (CORE-SVC-03); re-rate to 4 Substandard (CORE-SVC-07); check SLT-NC-02; pre-negotiation letter signed by borrower and guarantors (CORE-DEF-01).
2. Foreclosure: lost interest $346,950.00 + legal $60,000 + sale costs $150,000 = $556,950.00. Recovery $5,000,000 + $315,000 = $5,315,000. Loss $381,950.00, severity 7.4%. Deed in lieu: $231,300.00 + $35,000 + $150,000 = $416,300.00. Recovery $5,000,000 + $210,000 = $5,210,000. Loss $346,300.00, severity 6.7%.
3. Foreclose, unless the borrower clears the lien first. A deed in lieu leaves the $40,000 lien on title (CORE-DEF-04). NC power of sale is fast anyway (SLT-NC-01).
4. Not for an ordinary loss. The loan is non-recourse; the guarantors pay only if a carve-out is triggered (CORE-DOC-02).
5. $5,000,000 − $150,000 = $4,850,000 carrying value; charge-off $5,140,000 − $4,850,000 = $290,000.00.

**Common wrong answers.** Talking terms before the letter is signed. Comparing options without lost interest. Taking a deed in lieu over a junior lien.

## 6. Quiz: C13-quiz-v0

Pass mark 80%. Items marked (C) are critical. Each set has 12 items and covers all six objectives.

### Set A

1. (O1) The borrower calls to discuss a workout. First step? **Answer:** get a signed pre-negotiation letter (CORE-DEF-01). (C)
2. (O1) Who signs the letter? **Answer:** the borrower and every guarantor (CORE-DEF-01).
3. (O2) Maple Run, Ohio, 180 days of lost interest on $340,000 at 10.25%? **Answer:** $17,425.00 (F-CORE-05).
4. (O2) Why count lost interest in the comparison? **Answer:** time is a cost; slower paths lose more (CORE-DEF-02).
5. (O3) Name three conditions of a forbearance. **Answer:** any three of: written and time-limited; guarantor reaffirmation; all net income to us; taxes and insurance current; dated plan (CORE-DEF-03).
6. (O3) When may default interest be waived? **Answer:** only on full performance (CORE-DEF-03).
7. (O4) Title shows a judgment lien. Deed in lieu? **Answer:** not unless the lien is released; otherwise foreclose (CORE-DEF-04). (C)
8. (O4) What does a receiver do? **Answer:** collects rent, pays taxes and runs the property during foreclosure (CORE-DEF-05).
9. (O5) California trustee's sale; guaranty has no 2856 waivers. Risk? **Answer:** the guarantor may raise the borrower's protections (CORE-DOC-05). (C)
10. (O5) Indiana borrower asks us to waive the 3-month wait. Effect? **Answer:** no deficiency allowed (IC 32-29-7-5).
11. (O6) Loan $1,000,000; fair value $900,000; cost to sell $45,000. Charge-off? **Answer:** $145,000.00 (F-CORE-42).
12. (O6) How often is REO value re-tested? **Answer:** each quarter (CORE-DEF-06).

### Set B

1. (O1) A default interest bill: $1,000,000 at 10.00% + 5.00% for 30 days? **Answer:** $12,500.00 (F-CORE-31).
2. (O1) What rating does a loan in default with no exit get? **Answer:** 4 Substandard (CORE-SVC-07).
3. (O2) Option A loses $300,000; option B loses $250,000; no other factor. Which? **Answer:** B (CORE-DEF-02).
4. (O2) Loss $297,600 on $3,520,000. Severity? **Answer:** 8.5% (F-CORE-23).
5. (O3) Paydown leaves $3,220,000 at 8.25%; NCF $300,000. Coverage? **Answer:** 1.13x (COM-BR-02 needs 1.00x).
6. (O3) Can a workout be agreed by phone? **Answer:** no; only a signed written agreement changes the loan (CORE-DEF-01). (C)
7. (O4) Why does a deed in lieu not clear junior liens? **Answer:** it is a voluntary transfer, not a foreclosure sale (CORE-DEF-04).
8. (O4) Name the protection that stops foreclosure on a bankruptcy filing. **Answer:** the automatic stay.
9. (O5) Non-recourse loan, no carve-out triggered. Chase the guarantor for the shortfall? **Answer:** no (CORE-DOC-02). (C)
10. (O5) Deed in lieu with a creditworthy guarantor: what must the agreement say? **Answer:** the guarantee survives for the shortfall (T5).
11. (O6) Loan $3,520,000; fair value $3,400,000; cost to sell $102,000. Carrying value? **Answer:** $3,298,000.00 (F-CORE-42).
12. (O6) Who approves an REO sale offer? **Answer:** the approver for the original loan (CORE-DEF-06).
