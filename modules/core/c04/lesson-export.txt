  1. Hook (5 minutes)
  The broker's package for Cedar Court shows NOI of $575,000. Your underwriting shows
  $410,000. It is the same 50-unit building, and the gap is $165,000 a year.

  At a 7.00% loan constant, that gap is worth about $2.4 million of loan. By the end of this
  module you can rebuild the $410,000 line by line and say why the broker's number is not
  the one we lend on.


  2. Objectives
  By the end of C4, you will be able to:

     1. Calculate gross potential rent, vacancy and credit loss, and effective gross income (F-
        CORE-07 to F-CORE-09).
     2. Classify each line of an operating statement as income, operating expense, capital
        item, debt service or non-recurring.
     3. Calculate NOI (F-CORE-10) using the family underwriting rules for expenses and
        management.
     4. Calculate DSCR (F-CORE-11) and compare it with a minimum at full precision.
     5. Calculate break-even occupancy (F-CORE-12) and the cushion against current
        occupancy.

  3. Teach

  T1. From rent to effective gross income (Objective 1, 20 minutes)
  Gross potential rent (GPR) is the rent if every unit is leased all year (F-CORE-07).
  Vacancy and credit loss is the rent you do not collect: empty units, unpaid rent and
  concessions (F-CORE-08). Effective gross income (EGI) is GPR less vacancy and credit
  loss, plus other income such as parking, laundry or RUBS (F-CORE-09).

  Underwriting never uses a vacancy rate below the family floor. Multifamily uses the
  greater of actual, market or 5% (MF-GEN-05). Commercial uses 5%, or 10% for office
  (COM-GEN-05).

  Worked example 1: Cedar Court income (RD-MF-01)

     1. Formula. F-CORE-07: GPR = units × monthly rent × 12. F-CORE-08: vacancy = GPR −
        collections. F-CORE-09: EGI = GPR − vacancy + other income.
     2. Inputs. 50 units at $1,300 market rent; rent-roll collections annualized $702,000;
        trailing 3 months annualized $708,000; other income (T-12) $18,000.
     3. Substitution. GPR = 50 × $1,300 × 12 = $780,000.00. Underwritten collections = lesser
        of $702,000 and $708,000 = $702,000 (MF-GEN-05). Vacancy and loss = $780,000 −
        $702,000 = $78,000.00, which is 10.0% of GPR. EGI = $780,000 − $78,000 + $18,000 =
        $720,000.00.
     4. Result. EGI $720,000.00. The 10.0% vacancy is above the 5% floor, so no top-up is
        needed (D-CONV-09).

  T3. Underwritten NOI (Objective 3, 15 minutes)
  NOI = EGI − operating expenses (F-CORE-10). The family rules decide which expense
  figure to use. Multifamily and commercial take the greater of the T-12 and the appraiser's
  estimate, with management at least 3% of EGI (MF-GEN-06, COM-GEN-06).

  Worked example 2: Cedar Court NOI (RD-MF-01)

     1. Formula. F-CORE-10: NOI = EGI − OpEx.
     2. Inputs. EGI $720,000 (worked example 1); OpEx T-12 $298,000; appraiser's estimate
        $310,000, including 4% management.
     3. Substitution. OpEx = greater of $298,000 and $310,000 = $310,000. Management
        check: 4% of EGI = $28,800, above the 3% floor. NOI = $720,000 − $310,000 =
        $410,000.00.
     4. Result. Underwritten NOI $410,000.00. Using the T-12 would give $422,000 and
        overstate NOI by $12,000.

  Back to the hook. The broker's $575,000 is the stabilized NOI after the renovation (RD-
  MF-01 business plan: stabilized NCF $560,000 + $15,000 reserves). It is a pro forma. We
  size the day-one loan on in-place NOI and test the pro forma only at exit (MF-BR-08).


  T4. DSCR at full precision (Objective 4, 15 minutes)
  DSCR = income ÷ annual debt service (F-CORE-11). Core teaches it on NOI. Each family
  then sets its own income base: NCF for multifamily and commercial (F-MF-10, F-COM-08),
  gross rent for residential rentals (F-RES-07).

  Compare the unrounded ratio with the minimum. A DSCR of 1.2495 displays as 1.25x but
  fails a 1.25x test.

  Worked example 3: Cedar Court DSCR on NOI (RD-MF-01)

     1. Formula. F-CORE-11: DSCR = NOI ÷ ADS.
     2. Inputs. NOI $410,000 (worked example 2); bridge test ADS $358,050.00 = $4,340,000
        × 8.25% (F-CORE-03, C3).
     3. Substitution. $410,000 ÷ $358,050 = 1.1451.
     4. Result. 1.15x on NOI. On NCF, after $15,000 of reserves, it is 1.10x (F-MF-12), the figure
        the multifamily policy actually tests (MF-BR-07: 1.00x minimum).


  T5. Break-even occupancy and the cushion (Objective 5, 15 minutes)
  Break-even occupancy is the share of GPR needed to pay operating expenses and debt
  service (F-CORE-12). The cushion is current occupancy minus break-even. A thin cushion
  means a few lost tenants stop the loan from paying.

  Worked example 4: Cedar Court break-even (RD-MF-01)

     1. Formula. F-CORE-12: break-even = (OpEx + ADS) ÷ GPR.
     2. Inputs. OpEx $310,000; ADS $358,050.00; GPR $780,000. Economic occupancy 90.0%
        ($702,000 ÷ $780,000, F-MF-02).
     3. Substitution. ($310,000 + $358,050) ÷ $780,000 = $668,050 ÷ $780,000 = 85.6%.
     4. Result. Break-even 85.6%; cushion 90.0% − 85.6% = 4.4 points. Losing about 2 of 50
        units' rent would leave nothing for debt. That is why the bridge needs a rate cap (MF-
        BR-09) and a value-add plan.

  5. Practice (45 minutes)
  Work alone, then check the model answer. The case is RD-COM-01 Birchwood Plaza at
  stabilization. It differs from the worked examples in property type, rent, vacancy rule and
  debt (LD-05).

     1. Stabilized base rent $600,000 and expense recoveries $120,000. Vacancy is 5% (COM-
        GEN-05). Find GPR, vacancy and EGI.
     2. Expenses: T-12 $196,000; appraiser $204,000 including 4% management. Which figure
        do you use, and does management clear the floor?
     3. Find stabilized NOI.
     4. The term loan's ADS is $330,772.68 (C3). Find DSCR on NOI. Then find it on the
        commercial NCF of $434,000 and say which one the policy tests.
     5. Find break-even occupancy using the stabilized figures.

  Model answer

     1. GPR (F-CORE-07) = $600,000 + $120,000 = $720,000.00. Vacancy (F-CORE-08) = 5% ×
        $720,000 = $36,000.00. EGI (F-CORE-09) = $684,000.00 (no other income).
     2. Greater of $196,000 and $204,000 = $204,000 (COM-GEN-06). Management 4% of
        $684,000 = $27,360, above the 3% floor of $20,520.
     3. NOI (F-CORE-10) = $684,000 − $204,000 = $480,000.00.
     4. On NOI: $480,000 ÷ $330,772.68 = 1.45x. On NCF: $434,000 ÷ $330,772.68 = 1.31x. The
        policy tests NCF (COM-GEN-11, F-COM-08), against a 1.30x minimum for unanchored
        retail: passes.
     5. Break-even (F-CORE-12) = ($204,000 + $330,772.68) ÷ $720,000 = 74.3%. Cushion
        against 95% stabilized occupancy: 20.7 points.

  Build NOI in five lines

     1. GPR = units or suites × monthly rent × 12 (F-CORE-07).
     2. Vacancy and credit loss = GPR × the greater of actual, market or the floor (F-CORE-
        08; floors: 5%, office 10%).

     3. EGI = GPR − vacancy + other income (F-CORE-09).
     4. OpEx = greater of T-12 and appraiser; management at least 3% of EGI (MF-GEN-06,
        COM-GEN-06).
     5. NOI = EGI − OpEx (F-CORE-10).
