## 1. Hook (5 minutes)

Picture Maple Run twice. Same LLC, same $340,000 loan, same 10.25% rate. One house sits in Columbus, Ohio. The other sits in California.

In Ohio the loan is usury-exempt, needs no lender licence, and the guaranty can chase a shortfall. In California, an unlicensed lender charging 10.25% breaks a 10% cap. After a trustee's sale, it cannot sue the borrower for any shortfall.

Credit numbers do not change at the state line. Remedies do. This module teaches you to read the State Law Table before the term sheet goes out.

## 2. Objectives

By the end of C10, you will be able to:

1. **Determine** whether a loan needs a state lender or broker licence (CORE-BP-02, -03; SLT licence columns).
2. **Test** a rate, points and fees package against state usury rules (CORE-PRC-03; F-CORE-40; SLT usury column).
3. **Check** default interest, late fee and prepayment terms against state limits (CORE-SVC-03; RES-DSCR-11; F-CORE-31; F-COM-24).
4. **Estimate** how the foreclosure method and timeline change the loss (F-CORE-05, -23; SLT foreclosure column).
5. **Analyze** what recourse is worth after state deficiency rules (CORE-DOC-01, -02, -04; SLT deficiency column).

## 3. Teach

### T1. Licensing (Objective 1, 10 minutes)

Licensing turns on two facts: the loan's purpose and the property's state. Most state mortgage licence laws cover consumer loans only. A consumer loan is one primarily for personal, family or household use.

So a business-purpose loan usually needs no residential mortgage licence. Three traps remain:

- **Occupancy.** If a guarantor or relative will live in a 1-4 unit property, the loan can become consumer. Ohio's rule says so expressly (SLT-OH-01). CORE-BP-02 stops the file.
- **Commercial lender laws.** California licenses commercial lenders too, under the California Financing Law (SLT-CA-01).
- **Broker laws.** Indiana's Loan Broker Act defines a residential loan by its collateral, not its purpose (SLT-IN-01) [VERIFY].

**New rule CORE-BP-03.** Before the term sheet, check the property state's SLT row. Proceed only if we hold every licence the row lists or meet a listed exemption. A state with no SLT row gets no term sheet until one is added.

**Worked example 1: Maple Run, Ohio (SLT-OH-01).**

| Step | Check | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Purpose | Buy, renovate and lease a house. Business purpose (CORE-BP-01) |
| 2 | Occupancy | Vacant; to be leased to an unrelated tenant; Dana lives elsewhere. No red flag (CORE-BP-02) |
| 3 | Ohio lender licence | OAC 1301:8-7-02(G): a loan to buy a dwelling to lease is business purpose and outside ORC 1322. None needed |
| 4 | Ohio broker | Same carve-out. None needed |
| 5 | Decision | CORE-BP-03 met. Proceed |

**What-if: the same loan on a California house (SLT-CA-01).** A California Financing Law licence is needed to make the loan. The exception is a loan made or arranged by a licensed real estate broker. Without one of these, CORE-BP-03 stops the deal.

**What-if: Dana's sister will rent the Ohio house.** A relative in occupancy is a CORE-BP-02 red flag. The file stops until a credit officer clears it, and Ohio's licence carve-out may fall away.

### T2. Usury (Objective 2, 10 minutes)

Usury is a state cap on interest. Most states exempt business loans in one of three ways: by borrower type, by loan size or by lender licence. Check the exemption first; it usually ends the test.

To screen the price, use a simple all-in annual cost. It spreads the points over the term. It is a screen, not an APR, and it never replaces counsel's reading of the cap.

**F-CORE-40: All-in annual cost (SUGGESTED, new).**

1. Formula: AIC = note rate + (points % + other lender fees %) ÷ term in years.
2. Inputs (Maple Run): 10.25%; 1.50 points; 12 months = 1.0 year.
3. Substitution: 10.25% + 1.50% ÷ 1.0.
4. Result: 11.75%.

**Worked example 2: Maple Run usury test (SLT-OH-01, CORE-PRC-03).**

| Step | Check | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Ohio base cap | 8% (ORC 1343.01(A)). AIC 11.75% is above it |
| 2 | Exemption by size | Principal $340,000 is over $100,000 (1343.01(B)(1)). Exempt |
| 3 | Exemption by borrower | Business loan to an LLC (1343.01(B)(6)). Exempt |
| 4 | Decision | Any agreed rate. CORE-PRC-03 met. File the exemption reasons in the credit memo |

**What-if: California.** Article XV caps non-exempt lenders at 10%. Maple Run's 10.25% note rate is 0.25% over, before points. That is $340,000 × 0.25% = $850.00 a year of excess interest. The loan works only through a licensed lender or a broker-arranged structure (Civ. Code 1916.1).

**Checkpoint.** Why test the exemption before the rate? Because an exempt loan has no cap to test against.

### T3. Default interest, late fees and prepayment (Objective 3, 12 minutes)

Usury sets the ceiling on the note rate. Three other terms have their own state limits.

**Default interest.** CORE-SVC-03 sets note rate + 5.00%, capped by state usury. California adds a second limit. Its courts treat default interest on the whole balance before maturity as an unlawful penalty (SLT-CA-01).

**Late fees.** Our policy sets no late-fee figure [POLICY: late fee not set]. The states set caps for some loans:

- North Carolina caps late fees at 4% of the late payment, after 15 days, for loans under G.S. 24-1.1. Whether it binds G.S. 24-9 exempt loans is open [VERIFY] (SLT-NC-01).
- California caps them on owner-occupied single-family homes only (SLT-CA-01).

**Prepayment.** RES-FF-12 bridges have none. DSCR and term loans carry a 5/4/3/2/1 step-down "where allowed" (RES-DSCR-11, MF-TRM-06).

**Worked example 3a: Maple Run, 45 days late (F-CORE-31).**

1. Formula: default interest = balance × (note rate + 5.00%) ÷ 360 × days.
2. Inputs: $315,000; 10.25% + 5.00% = 15.25%; 45 days.
3. Substitution: $315,000 × 15.25% ÷ 360 × 45.
4. Result: $6,004.69. Normal interest would be $4,035.94, so the extra cost is $1,968.75.

Ohio check: the loan is usury-exempt, so 15.25% is allowed (SLT-OH-01). No Ohio statute caps business-loan late fees; "Not found" in the table.

**Worked example 3b: Maple Run DSCR refinance, prepaid in year 1 (F-COM-24 method).**

1. Formula: premium = principal prepaid × step % for the loan year.
2. Inputs: $338,000; year 1 step 5% (RES-DSCR-11).
3. Substitution: $338,000 × 5%.
4. Result: $16,900.00.

Ohio check: ORC 1343.011 limits prepayment on residential loans on 2 or fewer units to 1% in the first 5 years. That would be $338,000 × 1% = $3,380.00. Whether it reaches a business-purpose loan is open [VERIFY] (SLT-OH-01). Until counsel answers, offer the no-penalty option (RES-DSCR-11, +0.75%).

### T4. Foreclosure method and timeline (Objective 4, 12 minutes)

There are two methods. Judicial foreclosure runs through a court. Non-judicial foreclosure uses a power of sale in the deed of trust.

| State | Method | Typical time | Source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ohio | Judicial | 6 to 24 months | SLT-OH-01 |
| Indiana | Judicial; no sale until 3 months after filing | Not found beyond the 3-month floor | SLT-IN-01 |
| North Carolina | Power of sale before the clerk; 10-day upset bids | 60 to 90 days (secondary) | SLT-NC-01 |
| California | Mostly non-judicial; sale at least 3 months and 20 days after the notice of default | About 4 months or more | SLT-CA-01 |

Time is money. Every day in foreclosure adds lost interest, taxes and insurance. Count them as costs in F-CORE-23.

**Worked example 4: Maple Run defaults at maturity, Ohio.** C10 what-if facts: balance $340,000; legal and sale costs $28,000; taxes and insurance $680 a month (deal file); net sale proceeds $400,000.

Lost interest at the note rate (F-CORE-05):

1. Formula: per diem = balance × note rate ÷ 360.
2. Inputs: $340,000; 10.25%.
3. Substitution: $340,000 × 10.25% ÷ 360.
4. Result: $96.81 a day (unrounded $96.8056 used below).

Loss severity (F-CORE-23) at the short and long ends of Ohio's range:

| Line | 6 months (180 days) | 24 months (720 days) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lost interest | $17,425.00 | $69,700.00 |
| Taxes and insurance | $4,080.00 | $16,320.00 |
| Legal and sale costs | $28,000.00 | $28,000.00 |
| Total costs | $49,505.00 | $114,020.00 |
| Severity: ($340,000 + costs − $400,000) ÷ $340,000 | −3.1%: report 0.0% loss, gain $10,495.00 | 15.9% loss, $54,020.00 |

Same house, same price. The slow court path turns a gain into a $54,020.00 loss. That is why judicial states call for lower leverage or stronger recourse.

### T5. Deficiency and recourse (Objective 5, 11 minutes)

A deficiency is the shortfall after the sale. Whether we can collect it depends on state law and the guaranty.

- **Allowed:** Ohio, Indiana and North Carolina, with limits on homes the borrower lives in (SLT-OH-01, -IN-01, -NC-01).
- **Barred after a trustee's sale:** California (CCP 580d). Its one-action rule also makes us foreclose before suing on the note (CCP 726).
- **Fair-value defence:** North Carolina lets the borrower prove the property was worth more than the bid (G.S. 45-21.36).

CORE-DOC-04 requires this check before the term sheet. A non-recourse loan (CORE-DOC-02) recovers only from the property unless a carve-out is triggered.

**Worked example 5: Birchwood Plaza, Indiana (F-CORE-23; SLT-IN-02).** Library test facts: balance $3,520,000; costs $310,000; net recovery $3,150,000.

1. Formula: severity = (balance + costs − net recovery) ÷ balance.
2. Inputs: $3,520,000; $310,000; $3,150,000.
3. Substitution: ($3,520,000 + $310,000 − $3,150,000) ÷ $3,520,000.
4. Result: 19.3%, a $680,000 shortfall.

| Step | Check | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Indiana deficiency | Allowed (IC 32-30-10) |
| 2 | Trap | Barred if the borrower waives the 3-month wait with our consent (IC 32-29-7-5). Do not consent without counsel |
| 3 | Recourse | Full guarantee from Dana (COM-BR-07). Net worth $5,200,000 covers $680,000 |
| 4 | Decision | Recourse has real value. CORE-DOC-04 met |

**What-if: Birchwood in California, sold by trustee's sale.** No deficiency against the borrower (CCP 580d). Whether Dana's guaranty still pays depends on guarantor waivers [VERIFY: California guarantor waivers; counsel]. The lender may choose judicial foreclosure to keep a deficiency, at the cost of time.

## 5. Practice (25 minutes)

Use RD-MF-01 Cedar Court, North Carolina. Facts: commitment $5,140,000; $4,340,000 funded at closing; borrower an LLC; 1-month Term SOFR + 4.00% = 7.65%; origination 1.00%; 24-month term. Non-recourse with carve-outs (CORE-DOC-02). Do all five before opening the key.

1. Does the lender need a North Carolina licence? Cite the SLT row.
2. Work F-CORE-40 and run the usury test.
3. Month 6: the borrower pays 30 days late on $4,340,000. Work default interest with F-CORE-31. What does a 4% late fee come to, and does the cap bind?
4. Work 90 days of lost interest during a North Carolina foreclosure (F-CORE-05).
5. Can we pursue a deficiency against the guarantors? Explain.

**Answer key.**

1. No licence found. NC licences cover consumer-purpose residential loans only (SLT-NC-02) [VERIFY: no other NC commercial licence]. CORE-BP-03 met.
2. AIC = 7.65% + 1.00% ÷ 2.0 = 8.15%. Exempt anyway: the loan is $300,000 or more and the borrower is not a natural person (G.S. 24-9). Any agreed rate and fees.
3. $4,340,000 × 12.65% ÷ 360 × 30 = $45,750.83, which is $18,083.33 over normal interest of $27,667.50. A 4% late fee on the $27,667.50 payment is $1,106.70. The cap covers 24-1.1 loans; whether it binds this 24-9 exempt loan is open [VERIFY]. Our policy sets no late fee [POLICY].
4. $4,340,000 × 7.65% ÷ 360 × 90 = $83,002.50.
5. Not for an ordinary shortfall. The loan is non-recourse, so the guarantors pay only if a carve-out is triggered (CORE-DOC-02). If one is, NC allows a deficiency, subject to the fair-value defence (SLT-NC-02).

**Common wrong answers.** Running the rate test before the exemption. Counting default interest as recoverable loss in F-CORE-23 instead of lost note-rate interest. Treating "Not found" as "no rule exists".

## 8. Job aid: C10-jobaid-v0 (one page)

**State law check before every term sheet**

1. **Find the SLT row** for the property state. No row → stop (CORE-BP-03).
2. **Purpose and occupancy.** Business purpose certified; no guarantor or relative in occupancy (CORE-BP-01, -02).
3. **Licence.** Lender and broker licences held, or an exemption met (SLT licence columns).
4. **Usury.** Exemption first. If none, compare F-CORE-40 with the cap (CORE-PRC-03).
5. **Default, late fee, prepayment.** Default = note + 5.00% within the cap (CORE-SVC-03). Check the state's late fee and prepayment columns.
6. **Foreclosure time.** Add lost interest and carry for the state's timeline to F-CORE-23.
7. **Deficiency.** Can we collect a shortfall? Match the guaranty to the answer (CORE-DOC-04).

**Mini-example.** Maple Run, Ohio: business purpose, no licence, usury-exempt twice, AIC 11.75%. A 24-month judicial foreclosure turns a gain into a 15.9% loss.

**F-CORE-40:** AIC = rate + (points + fees) ÷ term in years.
